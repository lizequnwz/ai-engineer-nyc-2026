Expected lifespan and failure consequence are independent design questions. Short-lived work with bounded consequence can be simple and removable with clear scope, ownership and a retirement condition. Long-lived work adds stable interfaces, documentation and regression responsibilities. A short-lived high-consequence tool still needs access, numerical and execution checks, while a durable high-consequence system needs operated controls, recovery and continuing validation. Capital One’s conceptual durability discussion does not establish deployed disposable software or zero replacement cost. Cheap regeneration changes maintenance economics without removing the consequences of a wrong result. When an experiment gains users or dependencies, make an explicit maintenance decision about support, recovery and coverage rather than quietly allowing it to become infrastructure.
