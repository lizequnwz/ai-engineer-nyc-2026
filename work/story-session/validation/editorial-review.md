# Analytical-readout editorial review

Revised October 27, 2026 after the user requested a restrained consulting-style
engineering briefing with details, conclusions, and explicit takeaways.

## Presentation contract

- 22 fully visible analytical slides with conclusion-led headlines, supporting
  context, an engineering exhibit, and an application implication.
- Executive summary first; consolidated application questions and key takeaways
  last. The cases remain the supporting evidence.
- 35 minutes presenting, then a separate ten-minute discussion. The three finance
  cases receive nineteen minutes, Arize and Max five each, and summary/synthesis
  six in total.
- Flat tables and process exhibits replace decorative mockups, oversized values,
  dark scenes, and suspense-based reveals. The former reveal state machine and
  styling were removed. There is no alternate theatrical mode.
- The guide uses the same headlines and source bindings, with analytical speaking
  notes and focus guidance. No reveal cues remain.
- The five case openers now introduce the users, business problem, and system
  before the lessons. Every case footer includes the original talk title,
  presenter, and source application/workflow description. The executive summary
  also orients readers to what each team built or what perspective the talk adds.
  Kepler is described as a retrieval-augmented financial-research assistant;
  Vault as a shared research platform for people and agents; Contour as investment
  research workflows with evolving capabilities; Signal as an investigation agent;
  and Max’s talk as an engineering framework rather than a deployed application.

## Evidence and application review

- Kepler’s three failures, typed facts, deterministic human-written calculations,
  registry, and final filter remain distinct responsibilities. The architectural
  account is not upgraded to independent proof. The 17 example is a brief context
  note within the numerical-meaning analysis, rather than an opening device.
- The A17 example explicitly preserves $800,000 purchases, $1,000,000 redemptions,
  −$200,000 advisor net flow, and the separate +$5,000,000 firm aggregate. Both
  candidate outputs have one row. Main-slide checks now identify execution,
  row-count, entity/grain, and keyed-value outcomes. No commercial motivation is
  inferred from the numerical result.
- Vault’s Korean-won comparison remains a demonstration of temporal context.
  October 1–8 is example-specific. The eligibility table is our synthesis and
  separates task, authority, dates, and access.
- Vault’s roughly 200-question benchmark and reported 60% to 90–95% precision@10
  are visible, with relevance/accuracy/ROI distinctions and undisclosed held-out
  protocol. The human/agent table does not assume broad evidence guarantees a
  correct answer.
- The inbox story retains the precise claim that researchers still were not
  visiting every morning after improvements. The saved-search/collection mapping
  is the speakers’ explanation of product fit, without a measured adoption rate.
- Meeting preparation is an illustrative transfer to the user-confirmed emerging
  Salesforce context. Four questions now show evidence and acceptance conditions.
  The fictional client-concern case separates records, possible relevance, and
  hypotheses for professional validation.
- Contour’s neglected idea remains an emerging direction rather than a validated
  investment return. P&L impact is still to come. Comparisons are not portrayed as
  randomized. The accounting categories and accepted-task lens are our synthesis;
  no exact cost-per-useful-insight formula or causal ROI is invented.
- Arize’s Signal lifecycle remains vendor-described. The main slides expose the
  retained evidence and lifetime boundaries, then the judge-agreement matrix and
  a fictional false pass. No empirical frequencies, universal agreement threshold,
  autonomous deployment, or proved incident reduction is claimed.
- Max’s four factors remain consequences, dependencies, replacement cost, and
  maintenance cost. His argument is a spectrum, not a lifetime-only rule or a
  Capital One deployment case. Our application has no invented numeric risk score.
  Standards, embedded checks, deterministic tests, and service fakes explain how
  the cost of rigor can fall.
- The concluding synthesis is an engineering reflection, not a local audit,
  approved backlog, implementation sequence, or claim of transferred company ROI.
