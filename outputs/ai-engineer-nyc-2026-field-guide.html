04 / TURN ATTENDANCE INTO EVIDENCE

Capture a decision, not a transcript.

For each talk, aim for one reusable idea, one supporting example, one limitation, and one test. That is enough to recover the learning later without spending the whole session typing.

Name the decision.

DURING · 18 MINUTES

Find the mechanism.

BETWEEN · 30 SECONDS

Leave four breadcrumbs.

Takeaway → evidence → caveat → action. Then change rooms. Finish rough notes during lunch or the next break.

DAILY · 20 MINUTES

Three useful patterns, two unresolved risks, one experiment. Link each to a session; mark missing detail “unknown.”

Separate claims from conclusions

Use explicit evidence labels.

CLAIM

A statement from the speaker. Attribute it even when the slide includes an impressive number.

SHOWN

Workload, baseline, sample/period, and metric were described. This still is not independent verification.

CHECKED

You inspected the public artifact or method. Record version, date, and what it actually establishes.

IDEA

Your proposed application to Fidelity. Keep it separate from what the speaker demonstrated.

TESTED

Your team reproduced a result under its own conditions. No conference note starts here.

For every impressive percentage:
Percentage of what? Against which baseline? Over what period? Which failures were excluded? What did humans still do?

Questions worth a hallway conversation

  • Retrieval: How do permissions, stale sources, conflicting evidence, and abstention affect answer quality?
  • Evals: What failed after passing offline tests? Who labeled difficult cases, and how did you preserve a holdout?
  • Agents: What happens on timeout, partial failure, denial, or duplicate execution? Which actions need human approval?
  • Economics: What is the cost per accepted successful task, including review and rework?
  • Vendors: What can we reproduce in a small test, and which capability depends on a specific paid tier or deployment?

Three experiments to propose—not promises to make.

These 30/60/90-day horizons are options to discuss with your team. They presume neither approved datasets nor a particular vendor. Suggested sample sizes and a 20% time-saving hypothesis are illustrative planning choices; agree thresholds after measuring the baseline. Each layer should work end to end before you add the next.

30
Days / evidence-grounded assistant

Does better retrieval improve a useful answer?

Smallest useful test

Choose one bounded knowledge/research task on an approved corpus. Build about 100 expert-reviewed questions spanning answerable, multi-source, stale/conflicting, and unanswerable cases. Hold out final tests. Compare current/manual search with retrieval plus citations under the same access rules.

Measures

Expert answer quality, citation support, abstention, retrieval recall, stale-source errors, access failures, p50/p95 latency, review time, and cost per accepted answer. Hypothesis: ≥20% less median task time with non-inferior quality and no observed leakage in the defined suite.

Decision / stop rule

Deliver a reproducible benchmark and error taxonomy. Repair quality regressions or permission failures before any pilot. Zero observed leakage in a finite test suite does not establish zero risk. Compare reranking or memory only after the basic path works.

60
Days / controlled workflow agent

Can tool use save effort without hiding failure?

Smallest useful test

Add one repetitive, reversible research/reporting workflow once the assistant baseline works. Compare roughly 50–100 held-out tasks against the same manual or scripted process. Begin read-only or sandboxed; identify steps needing human confirmation.

Measures

Accepted completion, active human time, unauthorized/duplicate actions, recovery, interventions, trace completeness, latency, and total cost including rework. Inject timeout, malformed output, stale data, interrupted execution, and authorization denial.

Decision / stop rule

Deliver a working prototype, permission model, and failure report. Seek ≥20% less active human time at agreed non-inferior quality; stop on defined critical control failures. Increase autonomy only when recovery and controls work. Retain a deterministic workflow when it solves the task more simply.

90
Days / monitored pilot

Does the improvement survive real variability?

Smallest useful test

With appropriate approval, use a small, time-bounded cohort and shadow evaluation before consequential execution. Freeze versions, retain suitable redacted traces, sample real tasks, and define escalation and rollback.

Measures

Compare accepted quality, time, cost, incidents, and subgroup failures with baseline. A possible target is sustained ≥20% median time reduction over two weeks while meeting agreed quality, security, reliability, and budget gates.

Decision / stop rule

Publish grow / revise / stop with uncertainty, a reusable evaluation suite, monitoring runbook, cost estimate, and named owners. Show counts beside percentages for small samples. Do not scale on usage or satisfaction alone; report critical failure categories separately.
